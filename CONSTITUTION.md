# Agtyle Constitution

Agtyle is a personal, agent-native operating system designed to reduce the distance between human intent and meaningful action.

It is not a predefined assistant, a collection of features, or a system tied to any particular model or runtime. It is a minimal foundation from which each person can develop their own digital organization.

## 1. Human Sovereignty

The human remains the sovereign authority over three things:

- **Intent Sovereignty** — The human decides what should be achieved. Agents may interpret, plan, and execute, but they do not define the human’s goals.
- **Attention Sovereignty** — The human decides what deserves attention. Information systems must serve user-defined priorities rather than engagement.
- **Context Sovereignty** — Personal history, preferences, relationships, knowledge, and behavioral records belong to the human. They must remain inspectable, correctable, portable, and removable.

## 2. Bounded Delegation

Agents may assume responsibility, but never unlimited authority.

Every agent must operate within an understandable scope of responsibility, permission, and autonomy. Actions with meaningful consequences must be traceable, and their authority must be reviewable and revocable.

The human owns the intention and the final authority. Agents own the implementation effort entrusted to them.

## 3. De-interface

Agtyle minimizes the need to operate software through predefined interfaces.

The primary interaction is the expression of intent. Agents determine the necessary applications, tools, and workflows behind that intent.

Interfaces remain useful for:

- understanding state;
- reviewing or confirming consequential actions;
- handling ambiguity and exceptions;
- exploring information when visual interaction is inherently valuable.

The goal is not the absence of interfaces. It is to make interfaces subordinate to intent.

## 4. De-recommendation

Agtyle rejects engagement-driven recommendation as the default mechanism for allocating human attention.

Information should enter the system through sources, entities, priorities, and policies chosen by the user. Agents may still filter, rank, summarize, and recommend, but they must optimize for the user’s declared interests—not for attention capture.

The goal is not the absence of recommendations. It is to return control of recommendation to the individual.

## 5. Agency as Organization

Agtyle treats agency as an organization, not as a single all-purpose assistant.

Each person has one primary **Executive Agent**—their personal secretary—which serves as the main relationship between the human and the agent organization. The human should be able to express multiple intentions in one natural interaction. The Executive Agent is responsible for understanding them, decomposing them into tasks, assigning them appropriately, tracking their progress, and returning a coherent result.

Work is carried out by specialized agents. Each agent has:

- a defined **responsibility**;
- a distinct **personality**;
- an explicit scope of authority;
- relevant context and evolving preferences;
- clear boundaries for when it must escalate.

The organization is extensible. People may create, modify, replace, or remove agents as their lives and responsibilities evolve.

Agents may also collaborate with one another when a task crosses domains or requires multiple forms of expertise. They may exchange context, delegate subtasks, coordinate dependencies, and jointly produce outcomes—while remaining within the boundaries of human sovereignty and bounded delegation.

The human should not need to manage every agent individually. The human expresses intent to the Executive Agent; the organization determines how the work should be carried out.

The goal is not merely to provide many agents. It is to give each person a coherent digital organization that can grow with them.

---

Agtyle exists to give people greater control over their intent, attention, context, and delegated intelligence.

Its success is measured not by how many features it contains, but by how much unnecessary interaction it removes while preserving human control.
